The Critical Gap in Modern Disaster Response

Weather warnings stop at raw numbers. They do not trigger ground rescue.

Traditional Weather Warnings

  • Display rainfall in raw millimeters and cyclone tracking cones.

The Responder's Bottleneck

  • Disaster cells lack live data to prioritize boat dispatches.
  • Rescue personnel cannot verify shelter open bed counts.
  • Phone switches jam, cutting off vulnerable citizens.
  • Resource allocations rely on delayed manual phone calls.
Data Problem: Current portals output isolated atmospheric metrics with zero integration to civic shelters, road networks, or rescue fleet inventories.

AI Model 2 06 / 11

Module 3: Satellite & Drone Damage Assessment AI

Computer vision roof classification for urban search and rescue triage.

Dual-Temporal Vision Analysis

  • Compares pre-disaster and post-disaster optical aerial photos.
  • Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) detects roof damage.
  • Pinpoints bounding boxes around collapsed and flooded homes.
  • Extracts building centroid GPS coordinates for search squads.

Four Damage Classification Tiers

  • No Damage (Green): Roof intact; safe for temporary shelter.
  • Minor Damage (Yellow): Partial tile loss; safe structure.
  • Major Damage (Orange): Wall cracks; immediate evacuation.
  • Destroyed (Red): Total collapse; high-priority rescue spot.
Benchmark Dataset: Trained on the global xBD dataset (850,000+ building polygons under Joint Damage Scale).
Speaker Script (30 Seconds)
"Following a cyclone or cloudburst, emergency squads must identify structural collapse without delay. Our second model uses computer vision to inspect pre- and post-disaster satellite imagery. It categorizes building rooftops into four damage grades, from intact to collapsed. Search teams receive exact GPS coordinates of destroyed homes, allowing them to extract trapped survivors first."
